# Reference works

Ten printed works by Lella and Massimo Vignelli, broken into graphic elements, with how each carries over to digital and other media. Use them as benchmarks: compare output with the closest work and ask whether it would sit comfortably beside it.

## Knoll International Graphics (1966–1980)
Fourteen years of posters, catalogues, price lists and brochures for the modernist furniture company, with Knoll International set in Helvetica alongside Herbert Matter's 1947 K.

- **Type:** One family: Helvetica
- **Colour:** Process colours used as overlapping type
- **Scale:** A 1967 poster about 81 × 122 cm built from the company name alone
- **Layout:** Furniture shown as photographs and line drawings, with type kept quiet

Apply it:
- Let product imagery lead and keep type to one family, so the frame stays quiet and the product speaks.
- In a hero moment, use the name itself as the image (large, cropped, overlapping), but only where the name is already well known.
- Use the same tokens on marketing pages, price lists and docs so every piece reads as one family.

Sources: [MoMA: 20 brochures](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/89306); [Cooper Hewitt: 1967 poster](https://collection.cooperhewitt.org/objects/18734823); [Vignelli Center: 1967 poster](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/knoll-international-vignelli-massimo/bgHQVk36pMW8Og)

## Stendig Calendar (1966)
A wall calendar three feet by four, made of nothing but Helvetica numerals on a grid. Months alternate between black on white and white on black, big enough to read across a studio floor.

- **Type:** Helvetica numerals only
- **Grid:** A seven-column calendar grid
- **Colour:** Black and white, months alternating figure and ground
- **Scale:** Sheets 36 × 48 in, readable across a studio

Apply it:
- Let scale contrast do the work: one very large size for the key figure and one small size for labels, as in a KPI tile or a calendar view.
- Design in black and white first, and add colour only where it means something.
- Set numbers on a strict grid with tabular figures so columns line up.

Sources: [Vignelli Center: Stendig calendar](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/stendig-calendar-vignelli-massimo/aQHwHqYgKY7K0w); [Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/1289/stendig-calendar)

## American Airlines Identity (1967)
A logotype that ran American Airlines together as one word in Helvetica, half red and half blue, applied to everything from tickets and tags to uniforms and planes.

- **Type:** Helvetica, with American Airlines set as one word
- **Colour:** Red and blue split across the word
- **Identity:** One mark applied to tickets, tags, uniforms and planes

Apply it:
- Set the wordmark in a sturdy standard typeface so it stays legible at every size, from app icon to splash screen.
- Let brand colour carry a meaning, and apply it the same way everywhere.
- Refresh an established mark rather than replace it: Vignelli's principle of equity.

Sources: [Archivio Grafica Italiana](https://www.archiviograficaitaliana.com/project/309/americanairlines); [Dezeen, 2013](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=284369)

## NYCTA Graphics Standards Manual (1970)
The rulebook for every sign in the New York subway: typeface, colours, arrows, sign panels and how to make and hang them. It turned years of research into how people move through stations into one consistent system.

- **Type:** Standard Medium (Akzidenz-Grotesk), later Helvetica
- **Grid:** Modular sign panels
- **Colour:** Route colours; red and yellow kept for exits and warnings
- **Rules & bands:** A slim band across the top of each sign

Apply it:
- Write the system down: anatomy, spacing and usage for every component, as the manual did for every sign.
- Build screens from fixed-size modules, like sign panels, so new content slots in without a redesign.
- Keep red and yellow for errors and warnings only, never for decoration.

Sources: [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/nycta-gs-manual); [Standards Manual reissue](https://standardsmanual.com/products/nyctacompactedition)

## The Herald Newspaper (1971)
A short-lived New York weekly set entirely in Times Roman, on a grid of six columns with sixteen modules per column, one size for body text, and every page built from horizontal bands.

- **Type:** One typeface (Times Roman), one text size, italics for captions and decks
- **Grid:** Six columns, sixteen modules per column
- **Layout:** Pages built from horizontal bands; every headline fits one module

Apply it:
- Map the six-column, modular grid to CSS grid, with row heights set as multiples of the base line height.
- Use one body size across the product and size headings to fit the modules.
- Keep a second style, such as italic, for captions and decks only.

Sources: [Vignelli Center: The Herald](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/the-herald-newspaper-vignelli-massimo/YwE4FLwgZq4L5Q)

## New York City Subway Diagram (1972)
A diagram of the whole subway system that swapped tangled, geographic lines for straight routes at 45 and 90 degrees. Every route has its own colour and every station gets a dot: a dot on the line means the train stops there.

- **Grid:** Every line runs at 45° or 90°
- **Colour:** One colour per route, used as an identifier
- **Layout:** Geography abstracted so the network reads at a glance
- **Detail:** A dot on the line means the train stops there

Apply it:
- Snap connectors in flow and status diagrams to 0°, 45° and 90° so a complex system reads as one picture.
- Give each category one colour and use it everywhere that category appears: chips, chart lines, legends.
- Pair the abstract view with a literal one, such as a map or a table. Vignelli proposed a geographic map, a neighbourhood map and an index alongside the diagram, but only the diagram was produced.

Sources: [MoMA collection](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/89300); [New York Transit Museum](https://nytransitmuseum.org/vignelli)

## Bloomingdale's Brown Bags (1972–1973)
A new identity for the store's 100th anniversary, with its own typeface, Bloomingtype, and a family of kraft paper bags named exactly what they are: Little, Medium and Big Brown Bag.

- **Type:** Bloomingtype, a custom face from Avant Garde and Futura
- **Colour:** Kraft brown: the material is the identity
- **Identity:** Little, Medium and Big Brown Bag: one design in three sizes

Apply it:
- Name things literally. Plain labels like Little, Medium and Big beat clever ones in interface copy.
- A neutral, material-like colour can be the identity when it's used consistently.
- Make one component in a family of sizes rather than separate designs.

Sources: [Vignelli Center: Bloomingdale's](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/bloomingdales-packaging-vignelli-massimo/vQG5HRasXXcpIg); [Cooper Hewitt](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2016/05/04/the-art-of-shopping)

## National Park Service Unigrid (1977)
One modular system for every National Park Service brochure: a flexible grid, a black band at the top carrying the park's name, two typefaces and a limited range of sizes. Every format is cut from one standard press sheet with little waste.

- **Type:** Two typefaces: Helvetica and Times Roman
- **Grid:** Ten formats cut from one 12-panel base sheet
- **Colour:** Black band identifies the series; colour kept for maps and photos
- **Rules & bands:** Black identification band at the top

Apply it:
- Put a persistent black header band on every screen, carrying the product or section name in the same place.
- Derive a small set of page templates from one master grid, as the ten formats come from one sheet.
- Ship type as tokens: two families, a short list of sizes, and no one-off sizes.

Sources: [NPS Unigrid specifications](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/brochures/unigrid/index.htm); [MoMA collection](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/89304); [Vignelli Center: NPS](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/rgWh8PWDRfXQKQ)

## Institute for Architecture and Urban Studies (1978–1979)
Posters, the journal Oppositions and the newspaper Skyline for one of New York's leading architecture institutes. The posters opened with a thick black band, followed by rows of information bands in red.

- **Colour:** Red bands carry the information that changes
- **Rules & bands:** A thick black band, then rows of red information bands
- **Identity:** One structure across posters, a journal and a newspaper

Apply it:
- Use two weights of rule: a heavy band for the page's identity, lighter rows for the information.
- Let one colour mark the content that changes, such as dates and events.
- Use one structure across web, email and social, as the institute did across poster, journal and newspaper.

Sources: [Vignelli Center: Corporate identity](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/nwXBkY-mSyMkIw); [Design Observer: The Black Rule](https://designobserver.com/article.php?id=15488)

## The Vignelli Canon (2010)
Vignelli's own short handbook on design. Part one covers the intangibles, from semantics to equity. Part two covers the tangibles, from paper sizes and grids to typefaces, colour and white space.

- **Type:** About six typefaces, perhaps six more, and no more
- **Grid:** A-series paper sizes; gutters about one line of type
- **Colour:** Primaries, used as signifiers
- **Rules & bands:** 2pt between major parts, ½–1pt within

Apply it:
- Turn the tangibles into design tokens: typefaces, two or three sizes, grid, rule weights and colour roles.
- Use the intangibles as review questions, such as whether a screen can be understood without explanation.
- Set text flush left by default, with gutters about one line of type.

Sources: [The Vignelli Canon (PDF)](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf); [Lars Müller Publishers](https://www.lars-mueller-publishers.com/vignelli-canon)
